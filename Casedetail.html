ShiftCare: a human-centered roster and client system.

A people-first platform for Australia's NDIS providers, in-home aged carers and allied health professionals. This case study covers the research, the data model, one design system shared by a web dashboard and two mobile apps, more than 60 screens, and an honest audit of what I would improve next.

01 · The problem

Care is human. The paperwork around it isn't.

ShiftCare is a people-first SaaS platform designed to empower Australia's NDIS providers, in-home aged carers and allied health professionals. It simplifies employee and client management with a roster, task and communication platform tailored to regulatory needs.

This case study focuses on the roster management system, the user and employee profile architecture, and the task workflow. The aim was operational efficiency without losing human empathy.

Six overlapping challenges, grouped into three themes

01

Compliance

Staying NDIS compliant and handling certification and document expiry.

Design response. License expiry fields with validation, automatic reminders, and a single place to see who is expired.
02

Coordination

Managing multiple workers and clients, with geolocation and navigation to client sites.

Design response. A roster that links employees, clients and addresses, with a route to each site.
03

Record keeping

Logging shift-based activities on outdated tools such as spreadsheets and email chains.

Design response. Digital task, medicine and behaviour logs filled in during the shift, and a check-in and check-out by QR code.
We aimed to replace fragmented tools with a unified, digital-first platform optimized for field-based support work.

05 · Design system

One system for three products.

A web dashboard and two mobile apps had to feel like the same product. I defined one system in Figma: color styles, a Lexend type scale, and components with every state, built once and used everywhere.

01

Color

Primary blue and navy, secondary and extended colors for status, and a grey ramp for surfaces and text.

02

Typography

Lexend from H1 at 40 px down to inline text at 14 px, with line heights fixed per level.

03

Components

Inputs, dropdowns, buttons in six states, date and year pickers, PDF upload, pagination, pills and a task calendar.

Each control is documented in default, hover, active, typing and disabled states, so developers never guess. The mobile set adds larger touch targets and a bottom tab bar with a raised scan button.

06 · Design · Organization web dashboard

Built for the roster manager.

The web dashboard is where an organization manages employees, users, rosters, tasks and behaviors. A left navigation gives one section per record type. Every section repeats the same pattern: a searchable table, an add form and an update form. Once a manager learns one, they know all of them.

01

Add user and add employee flows

Fields cover name, contact, NDIS ID, license expiry and photo. Expired licenses are validated, documents can be dragged in or imported by URL, and password fields have a visibility toggle.

02

Roster flow

Assign several employees to one user or the reverse, choose date and time with smart constraints, group tasks, and set the location with an embedded map. Goals and tasks expand in place.

07 · Design · Employee mobile app

One hand, on the move.

Sarah is walking between clients with a phone in one hand. The app opens on the next thing she has to do and keeps the rest one tap away.

08 · Design · User mobile app

Visibility for the person receiving care.

James is a client. His app is deliberately lighter. It shows the care he has received, what the worker noted, and his budget, and it lets him confirm a visit with a QR code.

09 · UX principles in the product

Decisions, and the reasons.

PrincipleWhere it shows up in ShiftCare
Hick's LawThe mobile tab bar has four destinations plus scan. Web navigation has one entry per record type.
Jakob's LawTables, search and pagination look like every admin tool a manager already knows. Mobile follows bottom tabs and a calendar.
Fitts's LawCheck in is a full-width button placed at the bottom of the first card. The scan button is raised and centered.
ConsistencyEvery web section uses the same list, add and update pattern, and one design system serves all three products.
Recognition over recallWorkers select the client and item from dropdowns instead of typing them.
Error preventionLicense expiry validation, confirmation before adding an employee, and constraints on roster times.
Visibility of statusSuccess and error cards after saving, colour-coded task edges, and a visible running task.
Chunking (Miller's Law)Long client onboarding is split into separate pages, each with a single topic.

What I'd fix in the next round.

12 · Conclusion

Compliance and care, in one place.

ShiftCare's employee and roster management bridges the gap between compliance and care. Through a process rooted in empathy and supported by research, the result is a platform that is not just functional, but meaningful in the daily work of care.

01

Model the data first

A hand-drawn data model made the roster the center of every screen.

02

One system, many surfaces

Components built once kept three products consistent.

03

Never rely on colour alone

Status needs text and icons, especially in a job done on the move.
