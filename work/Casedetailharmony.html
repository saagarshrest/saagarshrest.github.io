Case study · Crypto wallet · Mobile app · 2020

Your keys. Your crypto.

Harmony Wallet is a self-custody mobile wallet for the Harmony (ONE) blockchain. You can create or recover a wallet, send and receive tokens across shards, pay with a QR code and stake ONE with validators. I designed the whole app in 2020: 38 screens across five flows, all built from one design system.

01 · The challenge

Self-custody puts all the risk on the user.

There's no bank to call. If someone loses their recovery phrase or sends to the wrong address, the money is gone. The design had to make people feel in control at every step without hiding how the blockchain works.

01

Keys are scary

New users have to write down 12 words before they can do anything. If that step feels like a chore, people skip it, and a skipped backup is a lost wallet.

02

Numbers have to be exact

Balances run to four or more decimals, addresses are 42 characters long, and every transfer has a network fee. All of it has to stay readable on a phone.

03

Harmony has shards

Tokens live on numbered shards, so a transfer needs a from-shard and a to-shard. That idea has to fit into a normal send form.

04

Nothing can be undone

A transfer is final once it's signed, so the app can never say "sent" until it really has been sent.

05

Staking is dense

Validators come with returns, fees, stake history and delegator lists, which is a lot of data for a 390px screen.

The brief. Design a complete mobile wallet that covers onboarding, recovery, sending, receiving, QR payments, staking and security. It should feel secure, calm and easy to use with one thumb, and every screen should come from one shared system.
02 · Goals and principles

Four rules behind every screen.

01

One clear action

Each screen has a single green primary button. Every other action is outline, black or grey.

02

Money is the hero

Balances sit on black cards in the largest, tightest type in the app, with the fiat value and daily change right underneath.

03

Thumb-first

Confirmations, fees and pickers open as bottom sheets. Key buttons are 56px tall and full width, inside the bottom half of the screen.

04

Say what happened

Every money movement has its own review, pending and success states, each with its own icon and wording.

03 · Screen map

The whole app on one page.

I mapped the app before drawing any screens. Four tabs (Wallet, Market, Staking, Profile) sit around a raised Send button. Each flow is its own stack, and every decision opens as a bottom sheet on top of the screen it belongs to.

04 · Design system · Colour

Green for action. Black for money.

The palette is small on purpose. One green means "do this" or "you gained". Black holds money and navigation. Neutrals keep forms quiet. Red only ever means a loss, an error or a log out. The Harmony logo keeps its own blue-to-teal gradient, so the product brand still comes through.

Every colour is a named token in a single theme, and no screen uses a raw hex value.

An 11-step primary ramp

The green runs from 5 (#EAFBF1) to 100 (#038E43) in ten even steps. Only four steps do real work:

  • 100 is the base: buttons, links and the active tab.
  • 90 (#179B53) is the pressed state of a primary button.
  • 10 (#B4FFD6) is the tint behind info notes and selected options.
  • 5 (#EAFBF1) is the background of chips, success rows and selected cards.

Accent pairs

Each accent is a background and text pair: HRC20 token avatars #FFF7CC / #8A6D00, warnings #FFF4E5 / #8A4B00, errors at 5% danger, and confirmed states at 15% emerald.

04 · Design system · Typography

One family, ten sizes.

Inter in four weights: 400, 500, 600 and 700. Its figures are clear at small sizes, which matters when a balance has six decimals. Money gets the biggest, tightest type. Everything else stays small and calm, so the numbers stand out.

Semibold is the workhorse, used 408 times across the screens for labels, buttons and row titles. Bold (109 uses) is kept for money and headings.

04 · Design system · Components

Built once, used on all 38 screens.

Five button styles, one input with three states, and a small set of selection controls cover every form in the app. Each component has a default and a pressed state, and inputs add focus and error.

Specs

  • Buttons are 56px tall with a 16px radius and a 15px semibold label. The green glow (0 10px 30px at 35%) is only used on the primary button, so it's always the first thing you see.
  • Quick actions are 48px tiles with a 16px radius, inside the black balance card.
  • Inputs are 52px with a 16px radius and a 1.5px ivory border. On focus the border turns primary with a 4px ring at 15%. On error it turns danger, and a message appears below the field.
  • Selection covers segmented tabs on a mist track, three speed cards (Slow, Medium, Fast) with fee and time, filter chips, a toggle, a select and a stake slider.
  • Labels sit above fields, never inside them, so they stay visible while typing.
04 · Design system · Patterns

Sheets, rows and states.

The bigger pieces are put together from those components. They repeat across the app, so a user who learns one flow already knows how the next one works.

01

Bottom sheet

A 32px top radius, a 40 × 6px ivory handle, a close button on the right and one primary action at the bottom. It's used for every review, fee breakdown, picker and confirmation.

02

Rows

Token, validator and transaction rows share one layout: a 40px avatar, a name with meta below it, and the value right-aligned. Gains are green, losses are red, and pending items are grey.

03

Feedback notes

Three tinted notes, each with an icon: info on green 5, warning on amber and error on danger 5. Every note says what happened and what to do next.

04

Stepper, PIN and recovery grid

A 3-step stepper (Name, Backup, Verify). Four 64px PIN boxes with an on-screen keypad. A numbered 3 × 4 recovery grid, so the order of the words is never in doubt.

05

Navigation bar

A floating black bar, 72px tall with a 28px radius, sitting 20px above the home indicator. Send is a raised 64px green button with a 6px white ring, so the main action of the app is always one tap away.

05 · Key flows

Five flows, 38 screens.

Each flow below shows four key screens and the decisions behind them.

Flow 01 · Onboarding

Set up in three calm steps

  • The onboarding slides use product-like illustrations (a price card, a QR transfer) instead of stock art, with a progress bar and Skip on every slide.
  • Creating a wallet is a 3-step stepper: Name, Backup, Verify. You can always see where you are and how much is left.
  • The recovery phrase is a numbered 3 × 4 grid with a security warning, plus Copy and "I've saved it" buttons.
Flow 01 · Access

Get back in, safely

  • The welcome screen is black with a soft green glow, the logo in the centre and a clear promise: "Your keys. Your crypto."
  • Recovery is one screen with two tabs, recovery phrase and private key, instead of two near-identical pages.
  • Unlocking uses four PIN boxes, an on-screen keypad and fingerprint unlock.
Flow 02 · Wallet

Money is the hero

  • The balance sits on a black card at 40px, with the fiat value, the daily change and a sync status underneath.
  • Send, Receive, Scan and Stake sit inside the card, right under the thumb.
  • Each token opens a detail page with a chart and its own history. Custom tokens are added from a contract address, and the app fills in the name and symbol by itself.
Flow 03 · Send

Review, confirm, done

  • Send shows the available balance at the top, puts token and shard side by side, and adds a MAX button and an inline balance error.
  • Network speed is three cards (Slow, Medium, Fast), each showing its fee and time.
  • A bottom sheet sums up the amount, fee, total and balance after the transfer before anything is signed.
  1. Review
  2. Confirm & send
  3. Amount sent · receipt

The rule. Nothing says "sent" until it has been sent. The receipt has copyable hashes and a link to the explorer.

Flow 03 · Receive and scan

Pay with a scan

  • Receive builds a request with an amount, token and shard, then shows a QR code on a black screen with the logo in the centre.
  • The scanner has green corner brackets, a moving scan line, a torch toggle and an option to scan from photos.
  • A scanned code opens a confirm sheet, so nothing moves until the user has checked it.
Flow 04 · Staking

Stake ONE with confidence

  • Validators opens with three network stats on a black card, then search, Elected and All filters, and a list sorted by expected return.
  • The validator profile stacks into cards that fit a phone: a summary with Delegate and Undelegate, general info and stake history.
  • Portfolio shows delegations at a glance. The delegate sheet has a slider and warns you before you stake your full balance.
Flow 04 · Rewards

Claiming has three honest states

  • Fees have their own tab, so the total is clear before signing.
  • Signing happens in an external wallet, and the app says so plainly while it waits.
  • After signing, the claim shows a pending state and then a final state with a link to the transaction.
  1. Fees
  2. Signed & sent · pending
  3. Rewards claimed
Flow 05 · Profile and security

Security you can find

  • Settings are grouped lists (Wallet, Security) with icon tiles, current values and a fingerprint toggle.
  • The recovery phrase is shown behind a warning, with the same numbered grid as onboarding.
  • A PIN mismatch turns every box red and shows a plain message. Log out opens a red-accented sheet that reminds you to back up your phrase first.
06 · Handoff

A design system that lives in code.

The app targets iOS and Android from a single React Native codebase, so the system was organised the way code would use it.

01

Theme tokens

Colours, type sizes, spacing, radii and shadows sit in one theme. Every screen reads from it, so a palette change, such as the contrast fix above, happens in one place.

02

Component library

Button, Input, Select, BottomSheet, PinPad, Stepper, TokenRow, ValidatorRow, StatCard and TabBar, each with variants, used across all 38 screens.

03

Navigation

A bottom tab navigator holds Wallet, Market, Staking and Profile, with Send as a raised centre action. Each flow is its own stack, and confirmations open as modal bottom sheets.

04

Native features

Fingerprint unlock uses the device's biometrics, and the QR scanner uses the camera with a torch toggle. Keys and the PIN stay in the device's secure storage, never on a server.

08 · What I learned

In a wallet, colour is a trust signal.

01

Less green, clearer screens

Keeping green for action and gain made every screen easier to scan.

02

Honest states beat polish

Separate review, pending and success screens did more for trust than any visual detail.

03

Check contrast early

The brand green looked strong but sat just under AA for small white text. A one-step darker shade fixes it, and it's cheaper to catch at the token stage.
