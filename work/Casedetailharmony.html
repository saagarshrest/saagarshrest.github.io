Case study · Crypto wallet · Mobile redesign · 2026

Your keys. Your crypto.

Harmony Wallet is a self-custody mobile wallet for the Harmony (ONE) blockchain. You can create or recover a wallet, send and receive tokens across shards, scan QR codes and stake ONE with validators. I designed the first version in 2020 and rebuilt all of it in 2026 as a mobile-first, production-ready UI.

01 · The challenge

It worked, but it looked like a prototype.

I audited all 36 screens of the 2020 version and found six problems that hurt trust, which is the one thing a wallet can't afford to lose.

01

Weak hierarchy

One bright cyan was used for buttons, links, headings, success messages and icons, so no action stood out as the main one.

02

Low contrast

Light grey labels on white and thin type made balances, fees and addresses hard to read.

03

Desktop-style dialogs

Confirmations, fees and rewards appeared as small cards in the middle of a faded page, out of easy reach of the thumb.

04

Confusing transaction states

The confirmation dialog already said "has been successfully sent" before the user pressed Proceed.

05

Inconsistent navigation

Back arrows, close icons and the bottom bar changed colour and position from screen to screen.

06

Dense data screens

The validator profile and portfolio squeezed tables and charts in sideways, and content was cut off on a phone.

The brief. Keep every feature and flow, but make the app feel secure, calm and current, using a green, black and white palette taken from Leapfrog Technology's brand.
02 · Goals and principles

Four rules behind every screen.

01

One clear action

Each screen has a single green primary button. Secondary actions are outline, black or grey.

02

Money is the hero

Balances sit on black cards in large, tight type, with the fiat value and daily change directly underneath.

03

Thumb-first

Confirmations, fees and pickers open as bottom sheets. Key buttons are 56px tall and full width.

04

Say what happened

Every money movement has its own review, pending and success states, each with its own icon and wording.

03 · Visual system

Green for action. Black for money.

The palette comes straight from the Leapfrog brand guidelines. Green signals action and gain, black holds money and navigation, and white keeps forms calm. The Harmony logo keeps its own blue-to-teal gradient, so the product brand still comes through.

Typography. Inter, Leapfrog's internal typeface, in four weights. Balances are 34–40px bold with tight tracking, body text is 15px, labels are 13px and small-caps labels are 11px with wide tracking.

One set of components for all 38 screens

  • 56px buttons in five styles: primary green, black, outline, soft grey, and red for destructive actions.
  • 52px inputs with a 16px radius, a green focus ring and inline error messages.
  • Bottom sheets with a grab handle for every confirmation, fee breakdown and picker.
  • A floating black navigation bar with a raised green Send button in the centre.
  • A 3-step stepper, a 4-box PIN entry with an on-screen keypad, and a numbered 12-word recovery grid.
  • Line icons at a 1.8px stroke, so every icon matches.
04 · Key flows

Five flows, 38 screens.

Each flow below lists the decisions that changed the most.

Flow 01 · Onboarding and access

Set up in three calm steps

  • The welcome screen moved to black with a soft green glow, the logo in the centre and a clear promise: "Your keys. Your crypto."
  • Three onboarding slides use product-like illustrations (a price card, a QR transfer, a currency list) instead of generic stock art, with a progress bar and Skip on every slide.
  • Creating a wallet is a 3-step stepper: Name, Backup, Verify. The recovery phrase is a numbered 3 × 4 grid, so the order is never in doubt.
  • Recovery is one screen with tabs for recovery phrase and private key, replacing two near-identical pages.
  • PIN entry gained an on-screen keypad with fingerprint unlock. A mismatch now marks every box red with a plain message.
Flow 02 · Wallet home

The balance comes first

  • The balance card is black, with the amount at 40px, the fiat value and daily change below it, and a sync status chip.
  • Four quick actions sit inside the card: Send, Receive, Scan and Stake.
  • Trending coins became swipeable cards with sparklines. My coins became tappable rows that open each token's history.

The home screen is the right-hand phone in the hero image at the top of the page.

Flow 03 · Send and receive

Review, confirm, done

  • Send shows the available balance at the top, puts token and shard side by side, and adds a MAX button and an inline balance error.
  • Network speed is three selectable cards (Slow, Medium, Fast), each showing its fee and time.
  • The flow now runs Review, then Confirm and send, then Amount sent.
  1. Review
  2. Confirm & send
  3. Amount sent · receipt

The fix that mattered most. The old dialog said the money was sent before the user had confirmed it.

Flow 03 · Send and receive

Pay with a scan

  • Receive generates a QR code on a black screen, with the logo in the centre of the code and Copy and Share buttons.
  • The scanner has green corner brackets, a moving scan line, a torch toggle and an option to scan from photos.
  • A scanned code opens a confirm sheet, so nothing moves until the user checks it.
Flow 04 · Staking

Stake ONE with confidence

  • Validators opens with three network stats on a black card, then search, Elected and All filters, and a list sorted by expected return.
  • The validator profile stacks into cards that fit a phone: a summary with Delegate and Undelegate, general info, a stake history chart and a list of delegators.
  • Portfolio adds a donut chart of delegations and a staked progress bar.
  • Delegate and Claim rewards are bottom sheets with two tabs (Details, Fees), a slider, and a clear warning when staking the full balance.
  • Signing in Math Wallet now has separate pending and success states.

Flow 05 · Profile and security

01

Grouped settings

Settings became grouped lists (Wallet, Security) with icon tiles, values and a fingerprint toggle.

02

Currency picker

The currency is chosen from a bottom sheet with radio-style rows.

03

A safer log out

Log out is a red-accented sheet that reminds the user to back up their phrase first.

05 · Built in React Native

A design system that lives in code.

The app runs on iOS and Android from a single React Native codebase, so the design system was set up in code from day one.

01

Theme tokens

Colours, type sizes, spacing, radii and shadows are all in one theme file. Every screen reads from it, so a palette change happens in one place.

02

Component library

Button, Input, Select, BottomSheet, PinPad, Stepper, TokenRow, ValidatorRow, StatCard and TabBar are reusable components with variants, used across all 38 screens.

03

Navigation

A bottom tab navigator holds Wallet, Market, Staking and Profile, with Send as a raised centre action. Each flow is its own stack, and confirmations open as modal bottom sheets.

04

Native features

Fingerprint and face unlock use the device's biometrics. The QR scanner uses the camera with a torch toggle. Keys and the PIN are kept in the device's secure storage, never on a server.

05

Platform details

Safe-area handling keeps content clear of notches and the home indicator, forms move up above the keyboard, and the floating tab bar reserves space so lists never hide behind it.

08 · What I learned

In a wallet, colour is a trust signal.

01

Less green, clearer screens

Using green only for action and gain made every screen easier to scan.

02

Honest states beat polish

Splitting review, pending and success removed the most confusing moment in the old app.

03

Code shows what mockups hide

Building the screens in React Native exposed layout problems a static mockup hides, such as long addresses, tables at 390px and content behind a floating bar.
